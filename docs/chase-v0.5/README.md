# CHASE v0.5: headfirst Superman dive, movement and physics contract

The **Up Arrow is a complete replacement** for the old upright forward leap. It launches a head-and-arms-first, full-body horizontal aerial dive through elevated slots. This is an exceptional athletic commitment, not an automatic vault or collision skip. Run, ordinary Space jump, momentum-only Down slide, and held Shift crouch retain their separate roles. Both Chaser and Evader use the same `ChaseMotor` and physics abilities.

## Launch, collision, impact, recovery

The shared motor launches in its **prior facing direction** at 9 m/s horizontal and 5.2 m/s vertical, with 22 m/s² gravity. Direction is committed, with no air steering and no wall-hit repropulsion. At 0.14 seconds after takeoff the actor attempts to transition from its standing capsule into a heading-aligned **solid non-trigger BoxCollider**, physically checking the full intended volume first. If obstructed, the actor remains on the original solid capsule, pays the failed-move recovery, and never tunnels through a roof. At least one solid collider is active at every step; continuous dynamic collision detection is preserved.

Full dive body dimensions are **2.30 m longitudinal × 1.20 m wide × 0.95 m high**, with local center Y **-0.26 m**, forward **+0.22 m** relative to actor center. They conservatively cover the actual imported head, hands, body and trailing feet, not just the torso. The collision box inherits the shared frictionless PhysicsMaterial so a shelf sill cannot permanently trap the athlete through contact friction. The visible full-body animation is Blender-authored, not added root motion.

If the airborne shape cannot enter between **0.14 and 0.26 seconds** after takeoff, it remains on the solid upright capsule and uses upright jump/fallback visuals instead of showing an unphysical horizontal body. Following physical chest contact, the player stays `DiveProne` for **0.40 s** and brakes. If there is not adequate standing room, it crawls forward at no more than **1.5 m/s** until its **actual projected upright clearance** permits rising. Physically raising the Rigidbody precedes collider expansion: the vertical crouch capsule is enabled without yet disabling the horizontal box. The conservative **union of two solid colliders** protects all get-up animation frames. At completion of the **0.35 s `LeapRecovery`** phase, the box is removed and normal standing-clearance rules resume. An upright/crouch collider is always present before removing the prone collider. Releasing and repressing Up does not cancel any committed or recovery phase.

`DiveProne` is a new motor state (appended to the enum to retain earlier numeric values). The 66-float future-policy observation stays the same *length* but changes normalization: movement-state value is `/8`, and recovery is `/0.75`, to cover both committed phases. Previously trained policies should **not** be reused without adapting these semantics. Active physics remains a shared Rigidbody, with normal physical tag-on-contact while diving.

## Purposeful geometry

The southwest competition obstacle is now actual two-level shelving, not another underpass: solid lower shelf rises **0.55 m**, solid upper shelf underside begins at **1.85 m**, and the opening between is **1.30 m high**. The upper panel and side supports are solid. The original northeast **1.64 m** low roof remains an independent crouch/slide route. A dive started at a correct distance has enough height to clear the sill and enough horizontal/vertical clearance to pass under the upper shelf; ordinary Space jump or Down slide cannot exploit the shelf slot. Mistimed and off-axis dives lose momentum against real solid shelf geometry, not a scripted failure animation. The separate `MovementGym.unity` includes a dedicated demonstration shelf with these same dimensions.

## Measured results and tests

Unity 6000.3.26f1 `Physics.Simulate(0.02)` in the actual PlayMode tests, not analytic estimates. Final baseline from [`physics-measurements.tsv`](physics-measurements.tsv): 9.00 m/s forward launch; 5.20 m/s vertical launch; approximately **0.5632 m apex**; **0.22 s to apex**; around **6.28 m** to the end of the combined airborne/impact/get-up interval in the unobstructed fixture. A 10-second continuous-run fixture travels **59.16 m**; using repeated dive attempts with default recovery travels approximately **49.43 m**. The preliminary timings are designed to keep an occasional correctly placed dive tactically useful rather than making dive spam optimal. This is a *gameplay tuning proposal*, not a verified real-human performance benchmark.

The new PlayMode regression suite checks actual dynamic colliders and contact, 90-degree heading alignment, no disabled collision frame, shelf traversal, ordinary jump/slide blocking, off-axis shelf support impact, wall impact/no replenished velocity, inability to stand under an overhead roof, completion of real chest/prone/get-up phases, real physical tag collision during dive, and baked imported vertex bounds against the collision box. The rig validator separately samples both elbows at **1/16 Blender frame** throughout all new dive actions. The exporter uses a 1/8-frame bake for accurate Unity interpolation, and the Animator contract rejects editor-only `__preview__` clips at build time.

The `Chase > Repair Production Character Animation Imports` menu picks up and rebinds all **12 real production FBX clips** (including `DiveProne`), and the pre-build asset validator enforces their frame ranges and loop flags. Preserve the single authoritative `Chibi/Chibi_Rigged.blend` and the 17 original skinned meshes; never recreate the character from older files.

Separate inspectable dive gym: `Builds/ChaseAnimationGymWebGL`, served with the same script on port `8766`. Browser builds **must be refreshed** after rebuilding; existing running Unity canvas state does not live-update. The server sets no-cache, gzip and MIME headers. For browser inspection, compare actual Chrome screenshots of Idle, Superman flight, prone contact, get-up, and standing recovery; don't infer from Animator state names alone. Capture evidence under `docs/chase-v0.5/evidence/`.

## Remaining artistic work

The 12-action Superman rig remains a stylized chibi model, not a real athlete's anatomy. Even with measured full-body clearance, animation subframe transitions, landing feel, opponent anticipation, shelf approach readability, and handling at varying browser refresh rates warrant human playtesting. Physics and collision tests do not establish that the maneuver is aesthetically final. Historic v0.4 documents and measurements intentionally retain the *old* upright leap for reference; this v0.5 file supersedes them for the active game.
