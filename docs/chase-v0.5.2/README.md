# CHASE v0.5.2 — committed impact instead of a blocked dive

**This is the current dive behavior.** The earlier v0.5.1 description of an Up Arrow launch being rejected near a wall or opponent is superseded. The user correctly identified the UX problem: a skilled athlete should be able to *attempt* a dive even when the chosen trajectory has no room. The obstacle or opponent must determine the outcome through contact, rather than an invisible move-eligibility check.

## Correct interaction contract

- **Up alone** attempts a Superman dive in the actor's current facing direction. **W/A/S/D + Up** aims at the held direction **on the takeoff frame**, then locks heading, forward speed, and aerial steering for the whole committed maneuver. This applies to Chaser and Evader identically.
- **Open runway or a valid aerial shelf opening:** the complete 2.30 × 1.20 × 0.95 m, non-trigger horizontal BoxCollider is installed on the takeoff frame. The 9 m/s forward and 5.2 m/s upward launch remains ballistic: gravity, contact, and the real Rigidbody determine its trajectory. Physical chest landing enters `DiveProne`, followed by the normal get-up and recovery.
- **Wall close to takeoff, side support, or opponent close in front:** the same Up press **still launches immediately**, without a `DIVE BLOCKED` message. If enabling the entire long box would overlap that obstacle, the actor uses its **already-solid upright CapsuleCollider for the attempted contact** rather than spawning the long box *inside* the wall or competitor. That capsule contacts the wall or competitor through ordinary PhysX. A forward wall contact cancels upward wall-climbing motion; real velocity is not replenished or teleported. Input remains locked until landing and the full **0.40 + 0.35 second** compact-impact recovery completes. The collider does not secretly expand through the obstacle or switch midair.
- **Visual consequence:** the compact contact uses the opening portion of `Leap` as a bracing forward attempt, freezes that pitch when forward contact is registered, then uses upright impact recovery rather than showing the fully horizontal body penetrating the wall. The camera and animation remain presentation-only; neither overrides physics.
- **Contact with another player:** diving into someone is **allowed and physically solid**. Contact uses the same match rule as running/sliding: **a Chaser contacting an Evader wins the tag**. An Evader colliding with the Chaser is also taggable—it does not award an escape victory or grant immunity. Tests cover both dive initiators at 0.95 m separation, where full-body spawn overlap would otherwise occur.
- **Under a low shelf:** if the player must crouch beneath the roof, they cannot initiate a standing Superman jump upward through that overhead solid. The ordinary `CanLeap`/upright condition still applies here, while crouch/slide passage remains playable. This is the intentionally retained exception.

A *solid physics collider* never phases through walls, and the real 17-mesh chibi continues to be animation-driven. As documented previously, the oversized chibi head and appendages sometimes extend outside the simplified standing capsule. This system prevents **collider overlap and long-box teleportation**, not an impossible guarantee that every deforming mesh vertex is collision-free at all arbitrary contact poses. A braced limited-pitch animation reduces that discrepancy rather than pretending a horizontal 2.3 m silhouette can occupy a 0.1 m gap.

## Regression coverage

Unity 6.3 LTS real `Physics.Simulate(0.02)` PlayMode tests now cover: wall at **0.58 m from the athlete center** (only a small gap beyond the original capsule), the full committed input/velocity despite that obstruction, real stop and no penetration, no wall-climbing, full grounded recovery without WASD steering or unintended move conversions, compact flight near a **side guard**, close-opponent contact in both role directions, and preserved tag rules. The separate low-overhead test confirms that crouched athletes do not launch through a shelf. All original shelf opening, forward projectile, normal collision, stun/recovery and regression tests remain active. Editor asset checks still require 12 real FBX clips, never `__preview__` clips, and unchanged official Blender geometry.

The independent Unity command-line Test Runner is the supported way to check this when the GUI Editor pipeline is unresponsive. See `Assets/Tests/PlayMode/ChaseSupermanDiveTests.cs`, `Assets/Tests/PlayMode/ChasePhysicsTests.cs`, and `Assets/Scripts/ChaseMotor.cs`.

The [original Blender source](../../Chibi/Chibi_Rigged.blend) was **not modified**. Its 12 animation actions, 17 skinned meshes, 20-bone source rig, approved proportions, orientation and feet pivot remain intact. The independent Blender source-layout and animation validators pass. Open-space Superman physics and animation remain as before; only **near-obstacle contact**, recovery selection, UI, gym staging, and focused regression tests have changed.

Do not interpret a win as guaranteed merely because one athlete initiates a dive. The existing **role-based contact tagging** decides the winner: the Chaser tags the Evader, irrespective of which player was diving. Subsequent human playtesting should still judge the impact pose and collision silhouette at varied wall angles and on different GPUs.
